Scan the sky your telescope can really see

Stand at your scope and sweep the phone slowly through 360°. MEBA Planner keeps a full azimuth × altitude sky map, including gaps under branches, and uses it to plan when a target is visible. A NINA horizon file is optional.

  1. Tap Start and allow motion/orientation and camera access.
  2. Hold the phone upright (portrait) at the height of your scope, a few feet from steel and the mount.
  3. Turn slowly all the way around, keeping the skyline in the middle of the frame.
  4. Tap Done to review the sky map, tonight’s target windows, and export. Use Manual mode if a direction needs a fix.

Your camera frames are analysed on this phone and never uploaded. This prototype works best in daylight or twilight on a clear sky.

Calibration & detection

Phone compasses can be off by several degrees, worse near steel. Sun and star calibration (aim at the Sun or a known star and the app computes the offset) is planned. For now, sight a landmark with a known bearing and slide until the readout matches.

Export for MEBA Planner

JSON and CSV are the azimuth × altitude grid the planner reads. Optional NINA .hrz is one altitude per direction (top of the lowest solid obstruction): Options > General > Astrometry > Custom horizon.

Prototype limits. Heading comes from the phone compass (iPhone: webkitCompassHeading; Android: absolute orientation) and can be several degrees off. Altitude comes from tilt plus an assumed camera field of view. Bright walls can look like sky; dark clouds can look like obstructions. Gaps under branches are kept when the branch is about a degree tall or more; thinner ones can be missed. Tonight’s windows use a simple sun and star calculation, so the times can be off by a few minutes. Always sanity-check the result against a landmark.
